Privacy Policy

Math Rotor 3D · Last updated November 2, 2026

Math Rotor 3D is a math game for iPhone, iPad and Android made by Jesahias Rodriguez (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what information is used when you play, who uses it and why, and the choices you have.

The short version

  • There is no account. We never ask for your name, email or any other personal details, and we don’t run servers that receive information about you.
  • Your progress and settings are kept on your device, and we never receive them. On Android, how the game is played, scores included, is also measured (below).
  • Ads keep the game free. Our ad partner, Unity, collects some information about your device to show and measure ads. Whether those ads are personalised is your choice, and you can change it at any time.
  • On Android, the game also uses Google Analytics for Firebase to count how it is played, such as games started and ads shown, and to measure our own Google Ads campaigns. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland it stays off unless you allow it. The iPhone and iPad version doesn’t use it.
  • Leaderboards (on iPhone and iPad) and the No Ads purchase are handled by Apple or Google.

1. Information kept on your device

To remember your progress, the game stores the following on your phone or tablet:

  • your best scores for each difficulty;
  • coins and gems, the balls and tunnels you’ve unlocked, and the ones you’ve picked;
  • your difficulty, practice, sound, music and steering settings;
  • when you last collected the daily reward;
  • your answer about personalised ads, whether you’ve rated the game, and whether you’ve bought No Ads.

This information stays on your device and we never receive it. Deleting the app deletes it. Apart from it, on Android the score of each game is counted by Google Analytics for Firebase (section 3; in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland only if you allow it), and on iPhone and iPad Game Center receives your scores if you’re signed in (section 5).

2. Advertising

Math Rotor 3D shows ads through Unity LevelPlay, Unity’s ad mediation service, which fills them from the Unity Ads and ironSource Ads networks. All three are run by Unity Technologies (“Unity”). To show ads, Unity’s software in the game collects:

  • information about your device and the app, such as model, operating system, language, time zone, screen size, network type and app version;
  • your IP address, and an approximate location (such as country or city) worked out from it;
  • device identifiers: on iPhone and iPad, Apple’s identifier for vendors (IDFV), and the advertising identifier (IDFA) only if you allow tracking; on Android, the Android advertising ID and app set ID, which you can reset or delete in your device settings;
  • how you interact with ads, such as which ads were shown, watched to the end or tapped, and any resulting installs;
  • technical information about ad loading problems and crashes in the ad software.

Unity uses this information to deliver ads, limit how often you see the same ad, measure how ads perform, prevent fraud and, only with your permission, show ads that suit your interests. Unity explains how it uses and shares this information, and the rights you have, in its Game Player and App User Privacy Policy.

Buying No Ads removes the ads that appear between games. Ads you choose to watch for a reward, such as a second chance, stay available.

3. Measuring the game (Android only)

On Android, Math Rotor 3D uses Google Analytics for Firebase, run by Google, to understand how the game is played and how its ads perform, so we can improve it. When it is on, it records:

  • events in the game: when a game starts and ends and its score, whether the ad choices card was shown and answered, and when ads start, are shown and how much they earned;
  • events Firebase records by itself, such as the first time the game is opened, sessions and how long they last, the screen in use, app and system updates, and in-app purchases (the No Ads product and its price);
  • information about your device and the app, such as model, operating system, language and app version, and an approximate location (such as country) that Google works out from your IP address;
  • an identifier Firebase creates for this installation of the game and the Android advertising ID: outside the EEA, the UK and Switzerland unless you have deleted it in your device settings, and in those regions only after you choose Allow.

It never records your name, email or anything you type. Google processes this information for us as a service provider; we don’t sell it, and we use it only in totals and trends, not to identify anyone. The one exception is our Google Ads campaigns, described next. Google may process it in the United States and other countries; where the GDPR or UK GDPR applies, Google’s data processing terms protect it with safeguards such as the European Commission’s Standard Contractual Clauses. Google describes how it handles it in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services and Privacy and Security in Firebase.

  • In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland (and on a device that can’t tell where it is), we measure only with your consent, which is our legal basis there (Article 6(1)(a) of the GDPR and UK GDPR). The game asks first. Until you answer, and if you choose No thanks, measuring stays off: Firebase records no events and sends nothing. Allow turns it on. If you later choose No thanks under Ad choices, measuring turns off again and the measuring information still on your device is deleted, together with Firebase’s identifier.
  • Everywhere else, measuring is on from the start, without using your information to personalise ads unless you choose Allow. If you chose No thanks on the card that asks about measuring, it stays off.

Measuring our Google Ads campaigns. We advertise Math Rotor 3D with Google Ads, and we link Google Analytics to our Google Ads account so we can tell which of our ads led people to open the game, play it or buy No Ads, and so Google Ads can show our ads to people more likely to play. For this, events such as the first open, games played and purchases, together with the Android advertising ID, are shared with Google Ads. Outside the EEA, the UK and Switzerland this happens while measuring is on, unless you have deleted your advertising ID; in those regions, only after you choose Allow. Under some US state privacy laws this may count as “sharing” personal information for advertising. To opt out, delete your advertising ID in your device settings (section 4), or write to us and we’ll help.

The iPhone and iPad version doesn’t include Firebase or any other analytics.

4. Your ad choices

  • The game asks whether ads may be personalised. On iPhone and iPad this happens when you first open it: iOS then asks whether the app may track you, and ads are personalised only if you say yes to both. On Android it happens when you first open the game in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, where the same question covers measuring, and after your first game everywhere else; ads are personalised only if you say yes. If you don’t, you still see ads, but they aren’t based on your interests or activity in other apps.
  • To change your answer at any time, tap ? on the game’s menu, then Credits, then Ad choices.
  • To turn tracking off for this or every app: on iPhone and iPad, go to Settings › Privacy & Security › Tracking; on Android, go to Settings › Privacy › Ads (or Settings › Google › Ads) and delete your advertising ID.

We pass your answer to Unity as your consent (or refusal) under the GDPR and UK GDPR, and as an opt-out of the “sale” or “sharing” of personal information under US state privacy laws such as the California Consumer Privacy Act. On Android it also goes to Google Analytics for Firebase as consent mode settings.

5. Game Center (iPhone and iPad)

If you’re signed in to Game Center, your scores are sent to Apple so they can appear on the leaderboards under your Game Center nickname. Apple handles your Game Center account and data under the Apple Privacy Policy. You can sign out of Game Center in the Settings app.

6. Purchases

No Ads is bought through the App Store or Google Play. Apple or Google processes the payment; we never see your payment details, Apple Account or Google Account. The game only learns that the purchase was made, so it can turn the ads off and restore the purchase on your other devices.

7. Children

Math Rotor 3D is made for a general audience. It isn’t directed to children under 13 (or the minimum age in your country), and we don’t knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child’s information has been collected through the game, contact us and we’ll help have it deleted.

8. Keeping and protecting information

We don’t run servers of our own that receive information about you. Information on your device stays until you delete the app. Unity keeps advertising information for the periods described in its privacy policy. On Android, Google Analytics keeps measuring information for the retention period we set, which is no longer than 14 months, and deleting the app ends the identifier Firebase created for it.

9. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct or delete personal information, to object to its use, or to withdraw consent. For information collected for advertising, Unity is the company that holds it; you can make requests through its privacy policy. For measuring on Android, Google holds the information for us. It isn’t linked to your name or contact details, and the game doesn’t show its identifier, so we can’t pick out one player’s information to show or delete it. You can stop it and remove it yourself: in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, choosing No thanks under Ad choices turns measuring off and deletes what is still on your device; anywhere, deleting the app ends Firebase’s identifier, and Google deletes the rest at the end of the retention period. You can also write to us about anything else and we’ll help.

10. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we’ll update this page and the date at the top. Significant changes will also be mentioned in the app’s update notes.
